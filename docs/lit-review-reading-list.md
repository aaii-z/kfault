## ⚠️ Read these three first — they decide your novelty claim

| Paper | Link | Why it is a risk |
|---|---|---|
| **robust-kbench** — Lange et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14279 | [232] Abstract states existing kernel benchmarks "suffer from **exploitable loopholes and insufficient diversity in testing conditions**". That is close to your premise. You must say exactly what they test and what they do not. |
| **CUDABeaver** — Li et al., 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08455 | [729] Abstract describes "**repair by degeneration**": a model passes correctness tests by simplifying CUDA into a safer but slower program. This is a *sibling* of your relocation finding — repair produces a fake success. Distinguish clearly: theirs is a **performance** loss, yours is a **correctness** loss that stays invisible. |
| **Kaizen** — Ludwig et al., 2026 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04058 | **GET.** Metamorphic fuzzing + differential testing of LLM-translated HPC code. Finds semantic errors that "compile and pass developer-written tests yet produce incorrect scientific results" — your silent category, on translation instead of generation. Strong support *and* a nearby claim. |

None of these kills your contribution. None of them studies **repair turns** changing the false-pass rate. But your gap statement has to be written after reading them, not before.
